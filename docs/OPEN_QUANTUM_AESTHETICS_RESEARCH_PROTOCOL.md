# Open Quantum Aesthetics Research Protocol

## Position

This document presents the project as an **open protocol for researching aesthetics in the quantum era**.

It does not claim that artistic concepts such as trust, forgiveness, beauty, or failure are physical quantum observables. Instead, it defines a reproducible research interface between:

- scientific visualization,
- contemporary art,
- human–AI co-creation,
- provenance and ethics,
- public participation.

The protocol is intended to make poetic interpretation auditable without reducing art to a single score.

## Core Research Question

> How can scientific data, aesthetic transformation, and public interpretation remain distinguishable, reproducible, and mutually generative within one shared protocol?

## Research Layers

### Layer 0 — Scientific Source

Records the original dataset, license, acquisition time, uncertainty, instrument, and stable source URL.

### Layer 1 — Transformation

Records preprocessing, mathematical mappings, software versions, parameters, random seeds, and output hashes.

### Layer 2 — Aesthetic Interpretation

Records the artistic decisions that are not scientific claims: color systems, composition, rhythm, metaphor, poetic annotation, and curatorial framing.

### Layer 3 — Participation and Trust

Records consent, accessibility, observer feedback, disagreement, revision history, and the distinction between anonymous participation and attributed authorship.

## Operational Terms

- **CoPhelia³**: an artistic and social framework for sharing failed attempts and converting them into dialogue.
- **RadicanTrust™**: a project method for building trust through provenance, transparency, revision, and reproducibility.
- **Quantum aesthetics**: artistic inquiry informed by quantum science, its instruments, concepts, limits, and public meanings. The term does not imply a new physical theory.
- **Failure**: a divergence between intention and outcome that may remain meaningful even when no correctable mistake occurred.

## Minimal Research Record

Every public experiment should publish:

1. source dataset and license,
2. exact data subset,
3. transformation parameters,
4. code or executable method,
5. software environment,
6. output checksum,
7. human and AI roles,
8. known uncertainties and limitations,
9. accessibility notes,
10. revision and dispute channel.

## Evaluation Without a Universal Beauty Score

The protocol rejects the claim that beauty can be reduced to one objective number. It instead uses a multi-axis research profile:

\[
\mathbf{A} = (R, T, F, P, C, X)
\]

where:

- \(R\): reproducibility,
- \(T\): transparency,
- \(F\): generative value of failure,
- \(P\): physical-data fidelity,
- \(C\): co-creation quality,
- \(X\): accessibility and public legibility.

These dimensions are reported separately. A weighted aggregate may be used for a specific experiment only when the weights and purpose are declared in advance.

## Next and Next Plan

### Phase 1 — Canonicalization

- Publish definitions and boundaries.
- Separate scientific claims from artistic metaphors.
- Create machine-readable provenance templates.
- Establish one canonical owner for each definition, dataset, and renderer.

### Phase 2 — Reference Experiments

- Gravitational-wave visualization from open detector data.
- Quantum microscopy visualization with perceptually uniform color mapping.
- Prompt-order experiment demonstrating non-commutative sequencing as a computational analogy, not a physical claim.
- Failure Phase Diagram study using observer responses and revision logs.

### Phase 3 — Public Reproducibility

- Release scripts, environments, hashes, and sample outputs.
- Run independent reproduction attempts.
- Archive failed reproductions rather than deleting them.
- Publish disagreement notes alongside successful outputs.

### Phase 4 — Research Community Connection

Prepare distinct contribution packages for:

- **Digital humanities and aesthetics**: failure, anti-optimization, authorship, interpretation.
- **HCI / CHI**: human–AI co-creation, trust calibration, consent, accessibility, observer studies.
- **Visualization / VIS**: provenance-aware scientific visualization and uncertainty communication.
- **SIGGRAPH / computational art**: reproducible transformation pipelines and generative visual systems.
- **AI research / NeurIPS workshops**: prompt ordering, model variability, evaluation under plural aesthetic criteria.
- **Open science communities**: reproducible artifacts, data citation, versioned methods, negative results.
- **Quantum outreach networks**: accurate public communication of quantum science through art.

### Phase 5 — Community Protocol

- Open a request-for-comments process.
- Accept multilingual issues and anonymous feedback routes.
- Define contributor roles and conflict-of-interest declarations.
- Publish annual protocol revisions with migration notes.
- Invite external researchers to challenge the protocol's assumptions.

## Invitation

This protocol is not complete by design.

Researchers, artists, engineers, educators, and critics are invited not merely to endorse it, but to test where it fails. A protocol for trust becomes credible only when its weaknesses remain visible, versioned, and open to revision.
